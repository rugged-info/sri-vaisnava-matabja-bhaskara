=== मूलपाठः ===


न केवलं पूर्वदुष्कर्मविपाकोऽपितु साम्प्रतिकचेतोमालिन्यमाह- कपटीति। एतेन  
गूढविप्रियकर्तृत्वं सूच्यते। चेतः कार्य वस्तुयाथात््यनिश्रयो नान्यहितघातकं कापट्यम्, आर्जवं  
हि ब्रह्मस्वभावः। न केवलं शास्त्रोपदेशेनैव, स्वाचारेणापि शिक्षयत्याचार्यः, स्वयमाचरते  
यस्मादिति तत्निरुक्तेः। मुखस्य जुगुप्सावहत्वात्सौम्यत्वनाशं वदति -कृष्णदन्तवान्। दन्तेषु  
काप्रण्य दन्तधावनाद्यभावजन्यं भवतु ताम्बूलादिभक्षण-व्यसनकृतं वास्तु. उभयथापि प्रमत्तत्वं  
विलासित्वं वा सूचयति । तादशदन्तकाप्रथात् प्रसादस्मितेष्वपि वेरूप्यकृतं भीषणत्वं जनयति।  
कुनखीत्यनेनापि पूर्ववदसभ्यत्वं गम्यते। साम्प्रतिकाचारदोषसन्ततिमाह बहुभीजनो बहुनिद्र  
इति। यस्तु स्वयं परिणाममननुसन्धाय प्रमाणाधिकं शुङ्ग, समधिकं शेते, तमोगुणप्राबल्यातस  
कथं साधकेभ्यो मितभीजित्वं प्रातरुलायित्वं वा शिक्षयेदिति भावः । व्यसनशब्दोऽनेकार्थकः,  
विपद्यपि दृष्टोऽत्र तद्योगादतिमात्रावसादशीलः, सर्वदा विपद्मस्तः, तमालताम्बूलादि-  
भक्षणव्यसनासक्तो वा कथं संसारदुःखनिमग्रशिष्यदुःखं व्यपोहयेदिति भावः। आचार्यानुगुणं  
हि मनःप्रसादः सौम्यत्वमित्याद्युक्तम्। मितं च सारं च वचो हि वामिताचायपिक्षिता, तद्विरुद्धं  
वावदरूकत्वमसम्बद्धबहप्रलापित्वं तद्रौरवमपनयति। ज्ञानवानपि यदि ज्ञानानुरूपं न चरति तर्हि  
कस्य दोष इत्यत्र भगवान् समाधत्ते जीवस्तु प्रकृतिमनादिकालप्रवृत्तवासनामेव यातीति  
शास्त्रकृतो निग्रहस्तस्य नियमनं कर्तु नार्हतीति। इथं शरीरवाङ्मयमानसदोषैर्मस्ता  
विद्रांसोऽप्याचार्यत्वेन नाङ्गीकार्यस्तिषां मुमुक्चुज्ञानोपदेशेन मोक्षसाधने युक्तत्वेन  
निषिद्धत्वादित्यलं दोषविवेचनेन।  
श्रद्धान्वितो विशुद्धात्मा मेधावी विनयी पटः ।  
कुलीनः सच्चरित्रश्च बुद्धिमान् व्रततत्परः ।।६७।।

na kevalaṁ pūrvaduṣkarmavipāko'pitu sāmpratikachetomālinyamāha- kapaṭīti। etena  
gūḍhaviprikartṛtvaṁ sūcyate। cetaḥ kārya vastuyāthātyaniśrayo nānyahitaghātakaṁ kāpaṭyam, ārjavaṁ  
hi brahmasvabhāvaḥ। na kevalaṁ śāstropadeśenaiva, svācāreṇāpi śikṣayatyācāryaḥ, svayamācarate  
yasmāditi tatnirukteḥ। mukhasya jugupsāvahatvātsaumyatvanāśaṁ vadati -kṛṣṇadantavān। danteṣu  
kāpraṇya dantadhāvanādyabhāvajanayaṁ bhavatu tāmbūlādibhakṣaṇa-vyasana-kṛtaṁ vāstu. ubhayathāpi pramattatvaṁ  
vilāsitvaṁ vā sūcayati । tādaśadantakāprathāt prasādasmiteṣvapi verūpyakṛtaṁ bhīṣaṇatvaṁ janayati।  
kunakhītyanenaapi pūrvavadasabhyatvaṁ gamyate। sāmpratikācāradoṣasantatimāha bahubhījano bahunidra  
iti। yastu svayaṁ pariṇāmamananusandhāya pramāṇādhiṁ śuṅga, samadhikaṁ śete, tamoguṇaprābalyātasa  
kathaṁ sādhakebhyo mitabhījitvaṁ prātarulāyitvaṁ vā śikṣayediti bhāvaḥ । vyasanaśabdo'nekārthakaḥ,  
vipadyapi dṛṣṭo'tra tadyogādatimātrāvasādaśīlaḥ, sarvadā vipadmas taḥ, tamalātāmbūlādi-  
bhakṣaṇavyasanāsakto vā kathaṁ saṁsāraduḥkhanimagraśiṣyaduḥkhaṁ vyapohayediti bhāvaḥ। ācāryānuguṇaṁ  
hi manaḥprasādaḥ saumyatvamityādyuktam। mitaṁ ca sāraṁ ca vaco hi vāmitācāyapikṣitā, tadviruddhaṁ  
vāvadarūkatvasambaddhabahapralāpitvaṁ tadrouravamapnayati। jñānavānapi yadi jñānānurūpaṁ na carati tarhi  
kasya doṣa ityatra bhagavān samādhatte jīvastu prakṛtimanādikālapravṛttavāsanāmeva yātīti  
śāstrakṛto nigrahas tasya niyamanaṁ kartu nārhatīti। itaṁ śarīravāṅmayamānasadoṣairmastā  
vidrānso'pyācāryatvena nāṅgīkāryastiṣāṁ mumukcujñānopadeśena mokṣasādhane yuktatvena  
niṣiddhatvādityalaṁ doṣavivecanena।  
śrāddhānvito viśuddhātmā medhāvī vinayī paṭaḥ ।  
kulīnaḥ saccharitraśca buddhimān vratatatparaḥ ।।67।।

Literal translation (with grammatical notes):

Not only the consequence (vipāka) of previous bad deeds (pūrvaduṣkarma) also, but the current corruption of mind (sāmpratika-cetomālinyam) is said (āha) to be deceitfulness (kapaṭi-iti). By this, the hidden harmful agency (gūḍha-viprika-kartṛtvaṁ) is indicated (sūcyate). The mind (cetaḥ) is to-be-done thing (kārya vastu), which, lacking proper foundation (yāthātya-niśrayaḥ), is no harmful (nāny-ahita-ghātakaṁ) deceitfulness (kāpaṭyam); honesty (ārjavaṁ) indeed (hi) is the nature of Brahman (brahma-svabhāvaḥ). Not only by the scriptures’ instructions alone (śāstra-upadeśenaiva), but also by conduct (svācāreṇa api), the teacher (ācāryaḥ) teaches (śikṣayati) and himself practices (svayam ācarate), because (yasmāt iti) that is stated (tat nirukteḥ).  

From the mouth (mukhasya), because of causing disgust (jugupsā avahatvāt), destruction of gentleness (saumyatvanāśaṁ) it says (vadati) – “having black teeth” (kṛṣṇa-dantavān). In the teeth (danteṣu), stinginess (kāpraṇya), arising from tooth-brushing etc. (danta-dhāvana-ādi-bhāva-janayam), let there be (bhavatu) a thing (vāstu) caused by addiction to betel-nut etc. chewing (tāmbūlādi-bhakṣaṇa-vyasanakṛtam). Both negligence (pramattatvaṁ) and playfulness (vilāsitvaṁ) too (api) are indicated (sūcayati). From such tooth-stinginess custom (tādaśa-danta-kāprathāt), even in grace and smiles (prasāda-smiteṣu api), deformity (verūpyakṛtam) terrible-ness (bhīṣaṇatvaṁ) is produced (janayati). Even by this (kunakhītya) previous civility (pūrvavat-sabhyatvaṁ) is attained (gamyate).  

A string of current conduct faults (sāmpratika-ācāra-doṣa-santati-māha) says (āha): much sleeping (bahubhījano), much sleep (bahunidra) thus (iti). But he who (yastu) himself (svayam) considering consequence (pariṇāma-anusandhāya), exceeds measure (pramāṇādhikam) pride (śuṅga) and excess (samadhikaṁ) possesses (śete), from tamoguna predominance (tamoguṇa-prābalyāt), how can (kathaṁ) to practitioners (sādhakebhyaḥ) moderation in drinking (mitabhījitvam), morning temperance (prātarulāyitvam) or (vā) teach (śikṣayet)? Such is the meaning (bhāvaḥ).  

The word “addiction” (vyasana) has many meanings (anekārthakaḥ). Though disaster (vipad api) is seen (dṛṣṭaḥ) here, because of that practice (tadyogāt) excessive despair nature (atimātra avasāda-śīlaḥ), always misfortune attached (sarvadā vipadmas taḥ), addicted to tobacco, betel-nut chewing etc. (tamala-tāmbūlādi-bhakṣaṇa-vyasanāsakto) or how (kathaṁ) worldly sufferings and disciple sufferings (saṁsāra-duḥkha-nimagra-śiṣya-duḥkhaṁ) would be removed (vyapohayet) such is the meaning (bhāvaḥ). The peacefulness of mind (manaḥ-prasādaḥ), gentleness (saumyatvam) congruent with the teacher (ācārya-anuguṇam) is indeed declared (iti ādyuktam).  

Moderate (mitaṁ) and essential (sāraṁ) of speech (vaco) indeed (hi), desired by the vomiting teacher (vāmitācāryā-pikṣitā), contrary (tadviruddham) rudeness (vāvadarūkatvam) disconnected excessive babbling (asambaddha-bahapralāpitvam) produces disgust (tadrouravam apanayati). Even a wise person (jñānavān api) if (yadi) does not act (na carati) according to knowledge (jñānānurūpam), then (tarhi) who’s fault (kasya doṣa) here (ityatra) the Lord (bhagavān) considers (samādhatte)? The living being (jīvaḥ) indeed (tu) only undergoes (yāti) the natural or beginning time prompted desires (prakṛti-manādi-kāla-pravṛtta-vāsanām eva). Therefore (iti), the control (nigrahaḥ) made by scripture (śāstra-kṛtaḥ), should not be imposed (kartum ārhatīti).  

Here (ithaṁ), free from bodily, verbal, and mental faults (śarīra-vāṅmaya-mānasa-doṣaiḥ mastā), even imperfect ones (vidrāṁsaḥ api), by teacherhood (ācāryatvena) not accepted (nāṅgīkṛtya), for those seeking liberation (artiṣām), by instruction in desire for liberation and knowledge (mumukcu-jñāna-upadeśena), appropriate (yuktatvena), due to prohibitions (niṣiddhatvāt), thus is the discrimination of faults (doṣa-vivecanena).  

Faithful (śraddhān-vitaḥ), pure-souled (viśuddhātmā), intelligent (medhāvī), humble (vinayī) scholar (paṭaḥ),  
Noble (kulīnaḥ), of good character (saccharitraḥ), wise (buddhimān), devoted to vows (vrata-tatparaḥ). (67)

Smooth English translation:

Not only the results of past bad deeds, but also present mental corruption is called deceitfulness. This indicates a hidden harmful nature. The mind, which must be managed, without a proper foundation, is not deceitful or harmful; honesty is indeed the true nature of Brahman. Not only through scriptural teachings but also by personal conduct the teacher instructs and practices himself, as stated in the teachings.

Because of the mouth's ability to cause disgust, gentleness is destroyed—one speaks of those with black teeth. Stinginess arising from tooth-brushing habits and addictions to betel-nut chewing and similar practices is acknowledged. Both carelessness and frivolousness are signs. Even from such tooth-stinginess, deformities and unpleasantness manifest, disrupting even friendly smiles. Yet, even this reveals former civility.

A succession of current conduct faults includes excessive sleep and inactivity. Whoever, considering consequences, indulges excessively in pride and tamas (darkness), how can they teach moderation or morning discipline to practitioners? The word "addiction" has many meanings. Even though disaster is seen here, excessive depression due to these practices leads one always to misfortune. Addiction to tobacco, betel nuts, and the like impedes the removal of worldly suffering and disciple’s suffering. Peace of mind and gentleness in accordance with the teacher are indeed advocated.

Moderate and meaningful speech is desired by the teacher of self-control. Contradictory rude and disconnected babbling causes disgust. Even the wise, if they do not act according to knowledge, are at fault. The Lord considers the living being to be simply following natural and time-driven desires. Hence, scriptural restrictions should not be imposed unnecessarily.

Here, even those free from body, speech, and mind faults, not accepted as teachers by the community of aspirants for liberation, due to appropriateness and prohibitions, should be excluded by the discrimination of faults.

Faithful, pure-hearted, intelligent, humble scholar, noble, of good character, wise, and devoted to vows. (67)
