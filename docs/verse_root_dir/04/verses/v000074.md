## Verse — मूलपाठः

```text
=== मूलपाठः ===
```

## Transliteration (IAST)

```text
प्राणायामः, स्वस्वविषयसम्प्रयोगाभावे चित्तस्य स्वरूपानुकार इवेन्द्रियाणां प्रत्याहारः,  
देशवबन्धश्चित्तस्य धारणा, ध्येयस्वरूपे देशो चित्तस्य विषयान्तरपरिहारेण यस्स्थिरीकरणं सा  
धारणा। तत्र प्रत्ययैकतानता ध्यानम्, ध्येयस्वरूपे हि प्रत्ययस्य ज्ञानस्य तदालम्बनतयैव  
निरन्तरमुत्पत्तिर्ध्यानं भवति। 'तदेवार्थमात्रनिर्भसिं स्वरूपशन्यमिव समाधिः" (३.३) इत्यादिना  
योगसूत्रकारेण यमाद्यष्टकस्य लक्षणानि प्रस्तुतानि, एतान्येव तादशध्रुवानुस्मृतेरङ्गानीति भावः।  
अत्राङ्गपदेनोपकारकपिति लभ्यते। एतेष्वनुष्टितेषूपकारकेषु ध्रुवानुस्मृतिः सपरिकरमनुष्टिता  
पुष्टा च भवतीत्याशयः। वस्तुतस्त्वीश्वरप्रणिधानेनेश्वरानुग्रहेणापि यमाद्यष्टङ्गं सफलं भवति।  
समाधिसिद्धियोगफलं वा सुतरां लभ्यत इति केचन तदनुग्रहेकबलास्तदेकशरणा भवन्तीति  
प्रावः।  
उदारकीर्तेः श्रवणं च कीर्तनं हरेर्मुदा संस्मरणं पदनितिः।  
समर्चनं वन्दनदास्यसख्यान्यात्मार्पणं सा नवधेति गीयते ।॥७४।।
```

## Smooth Translation

Literal translation (with grammatical notes):  
Prāṇāyāmaḥ, the breath control, (masc. nom. sing.), [when] the application (abhāva - absence) of union with one's own objects (svasvaviṣaya-samprayoga) is lacking, is the withdrawal (pratyāhāraḥ, masculine nom. sing.) of the senses (indriyāṇāṁ gen. pl.) conforming to the nature of the mind (cittasya svarūpānukāra iva).  
The binding of the mind to place (deśa-vabandha) and its concentration (dhāraṇā), the place (deśa) as the form of the meditation object (dhyeyasvarūpe), by removal of other objects (viṣayāntara-parihāra) is that firming (sthīrīkaraṇaṁ) concentration (dhāraṇā). There, the unity (aikatanatā) of the perception (pratyaya) is meditation (dhyānam), because in the form of the meditation object (dhyeyasvarūpe), continuous arising (nirantaram utpattiḥ) of knowledge (jñāna) of the perception (pratyayasya) by its own support (tadālambana) is meditation (dhyānam). The statement "'That only the meaning (arthamātra) is shining forth, like the very nature itself is samādhi'" (3.3), by the author of the Yogasūtras (yogasūtrakāreṇa), the characteristics (lakṣaṇāni) of the eight limbs (yamādyaṣṭakasya) are presented. These very are the eleven certain limbs (tādaśa-dhruva-anusmṛteḥ aṅgāni). Thus the implication (bhāvaḥ) arises. Here (atra), with the term "aṅgapada" as an aid (upakāraka), it is obtained. Among the practices that are beneficial (upakārakeṣu) and properly practiced (anuṣṭitāḥ), certain recollection (dhruvānusmṛtiḥ) with proper accompaniment (saparikara-anusṭitā) is firmly established (puṣṭā). The intention (āśayaḥ) is that indeed by the grace (prāṇidhāna) of the Lord (īśvara) and the Lord's grace (īśvarānugraha), even the eight limbs of yama etc. (yamādyaṣṭaṅgaṁ) becomes successful (safalaṁ). The result (phalaṁ) of meditation and perfection of yoga (samādhisiddhi-yoga-phalaṁ) is obtained (labhyata) more easily (sutarāṁ), so some say (kecana) that by the power of that grace alone (anugraha-ekabalāḥ), with exclusive refuge (tat-eka-śaraṇā), they become (bhavanti); this is the opinion (prāvaḥ).  
The noble fame's (udārakīrteḥ) listening (śravaṇam) and chanting (kīrtanam) of Hari with joy (mudā), remembering (saṁsmaraṇam), and reciting (padanitiḥ), worship (samarcanaṁ), salutation, service, friendship, self-surrender, these nine kinds (navadheti) are sung (gīyate).
