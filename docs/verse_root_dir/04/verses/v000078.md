Literal translation (with grammatical notes):
The dawn-time (uṣaḥ-kālaḥ, masculine singular nominative) becomes/is (bhavati, 3rd person singular present) (i.e. it is dawn). In that (tasmin, locative singular) stick/time division (daṇḍe, locative singular), having passed (vyatīte, locative singular past participle), who (yaḥ, relative pronoun) is four-stick-like (chaturdaṇḍātmakāḥ, masculine singular nominative) remaining (avaśiṣyate, 3rd person singular middle present), he (saḥ) is the time of sunrise (aruṇodayakālaḥ). At the fifty-seventh stick (saptapañcāśattama daṇḍe, locative), having crossed (atikrānte), who (yaḥ) is three-stick-like (daṇḍatrayātmakakālaḥ) remaining, he (saḥ) is the dawn-time (pratyūṣakālaḥ), thereafter (tataḥ), after half-sunrise (ardhasūryodayānantaraṃ), ended (śiṣṭaḥ), all (sarvaḥ) also (api) time (kālaḥ) is called sunrise-time (sūryodayakālaḥ procyate). As stated (yathīktaṃ), O knowers of light (jyotirviddhiḥ), "five plus five is dawn-time; six plus five is sunrise; seven plus five is morning; the remainder is remembered as sunrise" thus. Here (atra), it is said (nigadyata) thus. In the feet (pādāḥ, plural of pāda in meter) of action (karmakriyāyāḥ), the connection is (anvayaḥ). For the intended meaning of "five plus five measure" and so on, dawn, sunrise and morning are (uṣaḥ aruṇodayaḥ prātaḥ) are to be known thus, and obedience must be learned. In words of the arrow-speaker (bāṇavācakeṣu), the word is related to the number five (pañcasaṅkhyāparaḥ), thus applied to seven (sapteṣu upita), meaning "fifty-seven measured" (saptapañcamita).  
Four ghaṭīs indeed (ghaṭī catuṣkaṃ hi), is the name given to sunrise (aruṇodayābhidhaḥ), the chosen morning time (prātar vimṛṣṭaḥ), by great sages (maharṣibhiḥ).  
Thus here (tathā’tra), the chief Vedha (piercing knowledge) learned (vipashcitaḥ), have spoken (prāhūḥ), also the four portions (vibhāgāñ catuḥ), by discretion (vivekataḥ).

Smooth English translation:
The dawn-time occurs. In that division called a daṇḍa, after passing one, the time consisting of four daṇḍas that remains is the sunrise time. At the fifty-seventh daṇḍa, after crossing it, the time consisting of three daṇḍas that remains is the dawn-time. Then, after half the sunrise, when it is finished, all the time is said to be the sunrise time. As stated in the treatise on light: "Five plus five is dawn time; six plus five is sunrise; seven plus five is morning; the remainder is remembered as sunrise." Here it is declared thus. In the metrical feet of the actions, the sequence is established. From the intended meanings such as 'five plus five measured', 'dawn', 'sunrise', 'morning' must be known and obeyed. In the words of the arrow-speaker, the term relates to the number five; thus, applied to seven, it means fifty-seven measured.  
Four ghaṭīs indeed is the name given to sunrise, the chosen time in the morning by great sages.  
Thus here, the chief of Vedha and the learned ones have spoken about the four parts with discretion.
