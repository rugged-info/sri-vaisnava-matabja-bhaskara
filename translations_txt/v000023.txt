=== मूलपाठः ===


चेतनः प्राणी तद्यतिरेकेणाचेतनो जडः, ज्ञानाश्रयचेतनरमणस्य सग्वेऽचेतनरमणं कथ युज्यते। तत्रोच्यते- मोदते भगवान् भूतेर्बालः क्रीडनकेरिव' (हरि पु.२.१०२.३७) इत्यत्रेव चेतनाचेतनोभयवाचकभ्रूतशब्दप्रयोगवदत्रापि चेतनाचेतनपर्द युक्तम्। सद्वेषदर्थे नजचेतनतिर्यग्जातीयानां खगमृगादीनां विरहलीलायां स्थावरादिभ्यः स्वसंश्लेषदानेन च रमणं युज्यते। यद्रा चेतनः स्यात्सहदय इति कोषात्ससहदयानां भक्तिद्रुतचेतसामसहदयानामचेतनानां निसर्गक्रूरमनर्सा राक्षसानामपि स्वाभिरामगुणैः रमण इति। अतो 'रामः कमलपत्राक्षः सर्वसत्वमनोहरः' इति (२.६६.८) वात्मीकीयरामायणपद्ये बहुसतत्वमनोहर इत्यनुत्क्त्वा सर्वसततवमनोहर इत्युक्तम्। इत्थं भगवतो रामस्य स्वाधीनलीलोपकरणत्वमनितरचेतनप्रर्यत्वं च रमणे लभ्यते।  
स सर्वविधबन्धुत्वं सर्वप्राप्यत्वमेव च ।  
सर्वप्रापकता तेन तथा चोभयलिङ्खता ।।२२।।  
उच्यते तत्पदेनैव सच्िदानन्दरूपता ।  
यावद्िभ्ūtिनेत्त्वं श्रीरामब्रह्मणो मतम् ।।२३।।

Literal translation (with grammatical notes):

The conscious living being (cetanaḥ prāṇī, nominative singular masculine) is distinct from the unconscious (acetanaḥ) by that one difference (tad yatirekeṇa). For the enjoyment (ramaṇasya) of the conscious being, which rests on knowledge (jñānā-āśraya-cetanaramaṇa), how is the enjoyment of the unconscious (acetana-ramaṇam) joined (yujyate)? It is said (ucyate) there (tatra): The Lord (bhagavān) rejoices (modate) like a child (bālaḥ) at play (krīḍanakaḥ iva) in beings (bhūteḥ). Here (atra) also (api), like the use of words made fearful by the speech of both conscious and unconscious (cetanā-cetano-bhayavācakabhṛūta-śabdaprayogavad), the covering of conscious by unconscious (cetanā-cetana-parda) is appropriate (yuktam). For the purpose of good and evil (sadveṣa-arthē), in the separation-play (virahilīlāyām) of living, nonliving, obliquely related species (naja-cetana-tiryag-jātīyānām), of birds and animals (khagamṛgādīnām), as well as immovables and others (sthāvarādibhyḥ), enjoyment (ramaṇam) is joined (yujyate) by the giving of self-combining (svasamślēṣadānena ca). Because if the conscious being (cetanaḥ) is compassionate (sahadaya), so it is that, from the treasury (koṣāt) of compassionate ones (sasahadayanām), of devotees with quick mind and compassionate ones among the unconscious (bhaktidrutacetasāmasahadayānām acetānām), even the cruel enemies of nature (nisargakrūrāmanarsā) among demons (rākṣasānām), enjoy (ramaṇa) with pleasing qualities (svābhirāmaguṇaiḥ). Therefore (ato) 'Rāma, lotus-petal-eyed, charming to all beings' (rāmaḥ kamalapatrākṣaḥ sarvasatvamanoḥraḥ) is said (iti) in Valmiki's Rāmāyaṇa (2.66.8), having uttered many-beings-charming (bahusatvamanoḥra) and many-beings-charming (sarvasatatvamanoḥra). Thus (itthaṃ), the independent play-instrumentality and non-different conscious variety (svādhīnalīlopakaraṇatvamanitaracetanapraratyam) of the Lord Rāma is obtained in enjoyment (ramaṇe labhyate).  
He (sa) is all-kind of kinship (sarvavidhabandhutvaṃ), and also undoubtedly all-attainability (sarvaprapyatvameva).  
All attainability, by him, likewise is marked by both qualities (sarvaprapakatā tena tathā cōbhayaliṅkhatā). (Verse 22)  
It is said (ucyate) by that very word (tat-padenaiva), the nature of consciousness, existence, and bliss (sacitānandarūpatā).  
As long as this is the appearance (yāvadibbhūtinetvam) — this is the view (matam) of Śrī Rāma Brahman. (Verse 23)

Smooth English translation:

The conscious living being is distinguished from the unconscious by that single difference. How then is the enjoyment of the unconscious joined with the enjoyment of conscious beings founded on knowledge? It is said: The Lord rejoices like a child playing among beings. Here, even as words expressing both conscious and unconscious states are used, it is appropriate that unconsciousness covers consciousness. For the purpose of good and evil, in the play of separation among living, nonliving, and indirectly related species such as birds and animals, as well as immovable things, enjoyment arises through the giving of self-union. If the conscious being is compassionate, then from the treasury of compassionate devotees, even the cruel enemies of nature among demons enjoy with their own pleasing qualities. Therefore, it is said in Valmiki’s Rāmāyaṇa: "Rāma, lotus-petal-eyed, charming to all beings," uttering "charming many beings" and "charming all beings." Thus, the independent instrumentality of Lord Rāma’s play and the inseparable conscious variety is realized in enjoyment.  
He possesses all kinds of kinship and indeed all attainability.  
All attainability by him is likewise marked by both aspects. (Verse 22)  
It is said by that very word the nature of existence, consciousness, and bliss,  
As long as this appearance continues — this is the view of Śrī Rāma Brahman. (Verse 23)
