१३२ श्रीवैष्णवमतान्नभास्करः  
हृदयत्वं यद्ोषरौक्ष्यातिगं निजे। जने स्यात्तद्धि वात्सल्यं भक्ते प्राणस्य व हरेः।।  
ममतामोहसम्पको द्रदीयांस्तनुजादिषु। यप्पिच्छलमनस्कत्वं विदुर्वात्सल्य-मुत्तमाः ।। वत्सः  
स्रेहगुणस्थमास्तद्रत्ता वत्सलो हरिः' इति। क्षमया पृथिवी समः' (वा.रा.१.१.१८) इति  
भगवत्युक्ताया क्षमायाः पराकाष्टैव वात्सल्यमतो नान्तरीयकतया सा तु सुतरामेवोच्यते। स्थाने  
चकारः पूर्वोक्तिार्थन सह संवादीत्य-व्यवहितवक्तव्त्वाद्धगवतोऽपि वात्सल्यस्येति द्योतयति  
तत्सजातीयवस्तुगणसमाकलन-व्यूयतां च ध्वनयति। तथाविधवात्सल्यस्य नित्याश्रयमाह-  
विभीर्वात्सल्यमहार्णवस्येति पदद्रयेन।  
तत्रापि प्रथमं प्रियो विपुतां संस्थाप्याव्यवधानेन भगवतोऽपि विशेषणद्वारेण तत््वमुच्यते -  
विभीरिति। सर्वमूर्तसंयोगिन इत्यर्थः, देशकालाद्यपरिच्छेदेन सर्वत्र स्फूर्तिमुत्तत्वा  
वात्सल्यानुगुणं सामर्ध्यमुक्तं भवति, न ह्यकदेशी वत्सलोऽपि देशान्तरस्थजनदोषनिराकरणे  
तदङ्गीकरणे तद्रक्षणे वा प्रभवेदिति तादृशस्य तद् न सर्वत्र सफलं भवति। नित्यज्ञानत्वेन  
सर्वगतत्वेन च भगवतस्तत्सफलं भवति। न प्राकृतो जनो जीवस्य जन्ममरणजरादिदुःखं  
विज्ञायापि परिहर्तुं शक्नोति, न हि तुषतण्डुलवत्ते दोषाः स्वाल्पश्रमेण शक्यपरिहारा इति  
भावः। अत उक्तं 'स रक्षिता रक्षति यो हि गर्भं (भा.पु. ७.२.३८) इति। 'गुरवो दीनवत्सलाः  
इत्युक्तेः सत्त्वशुद्धेः सामर्थ्यस्य च परिमितत्वेन नित्यशुद्ध -स्यानन्तसामर्ध्यस्य भगवतश्च  
तदपरिमेयमित्याह वातससल्यमहार्णवस्येति। महार्णव-कथनेनाश्रितस्य सर्वेऽपि दोषास्तत्र निमग्रा  
भवन्तीति ध्वन्यते। वस्तुतोऽन्वर्थतश्च वात्सल्यं भगवत्येव घटतेऽन्यत्र तत्प्रयोगस्तु तद्  
गुणलेशयोगान्द्राक्त एवेति पदयपूर्वार्धस्य विवक्षितम्। एवम्भूतवात्सल्यस्य भगवदेकाश्रयताया  
वक्तरि प्रामाण्यमुच्यते - समुच्यत इत्यादयुत्तरार्धन । अस्वतन्तकैर्भगवदधीनताप्रत्ययवद्धिः  
सदाचारपरायणेः शास्तविहित-कर्मतत्परैवरैर्शानतो वयसा तपसा च वृद्धत्वच्छरष्ैनृभिरिति ।  
इत्थं शास्तरवश्यत्वबुद्धया कर्मनिष्ठा तत्प्रवृत्तिश्च तया च वरत्व॑मुत्कृष्टतया सर्वमान्यत्वमिति मिथी  
हेतुपरम्परा, तेन तदुच्यमानस्याविगीतत्वं सूच्यते। परायणशब्दादेव नित्यावलम्बत्वप्रतीत्या  
विभीः सदा वात्सल्यमीष्टमभि-मतमि्यर्थसिद्धये सदाशब्दस्य पूर्वार्धिऽन्वय उचितः। यथा लोके  
माता वात्सल्यात्पुत्र-दोषान्नविगणस्य सह्यति तच्छरीरगतमलादिकमपसाययपिक्षय  
वार्लिष्यति, तथैव भगवाञ् शरणागतजीवांस्तद्रदोषोपेक्षया तात्रिवार्य वा स्वीकर्तुं व्यग्रो  
"वतीत्याशयः। सर्वदोष-निराकरणस्य तदुपेक्षाया वा सामर्थ्यस्य भगवत्येव सत्वाद्रात्सल्यं  
तन्तरिष्ठमेवेति भावः।  
दयाऽन्यदुःखस्य निगद्यते बुधेरप्राकृतेस्तैरसहिष्णुता स्मृता ।  
कृपामहान्धेः समुदारकीर्तर्विष्णोरचिन्त्याखिलवैभवस्य वे ।।१०८।।

132 śrīvaiṣṇvamatānnabhāskaraḥ  
hṛdayatvaṃ yadoṣarauksyātigaṃ nije। jane syāttaddhi vātsalyaṃ bhakte prāṇasya va hareḥ।।  
mamatāmohasampako dradīyāṃstanujādiṣu। yappicchalamanaskatvaṃ vidurvātsalya-muttamāḥ।। vatsaḥ  
srehaguṇa-sthamāstadrattā vatsalo hariḥ' iti। kṣamayā pṛthivī samaḥ' (vā.rā.1.1.18) iti  
bhagavatyuktāyā kṣamāyāḥ parākāṣṭaiva vātsalyamato nāntarīyakatayā sā tu sutaramevocyate। sthāne  
cakāraḥ pūrvoktiārthana saha saṃvādītya-vyavahita-vaktavtvāddhagavato'pi vātsalyasyeti dyotayati  
tatsajātiyavastugaṇasamākalana-vyūyatāṃ ca dhvanayati। tathāvidhavātsalyasya nityāśrayamāha-  
vibhīrvātsalyamahārṇavasyeti padadrayena।  
tatrāpi prathamaṃ priyo viputāṃ saṃsthāpyāvyavadhānena bhagavato'pi viśeṣaṇadvāreṇa tatvamuccyate -  
vibhīriti। sarvamūrthasaṃyogina ityarthaḥ, deśakālādyapariccheden sarvatra sphūrtimuttatvā  
vātsalyānuguṇaṃ sāmardhyamuktaṃ bhavati, na hyakadeśī vatsalo'pi deśāntarasthajanadoṣanirākaraṇe  
tadaṅgīkaraṇe tadrakṣaṇe vā prabhavediti tādṛśasya tad na sarvatra saphalaṃ bhavati। nityajñānatvena  
sarvagatvena ca bhagavastatsaphalaṃ bhavati। na prākṛto jano jīvasya janmamaraṇajarādiduḥkhaṃ  
vijñyāyapi parihartuṃ śaknoti, na hi tuṣataṇḍulavatte doṣāḥ svālpaśrameṇa śakyaparihārā iti  
bhāvaḥ। ata uktaṃ 'sa rakṣitā rakṣati yo hi garbhaṃ (bhā.pu. 7.2.38) iti। 'guravo dīnavatsalāḥ  
ityukteḥ sattvaśuddheḥ sāmarthyasya ca parimitvena nityaśuddha-syānantasāmardhyasya bhagavataśca  
tadaparimeyamityāha vātasalyamahārṇavasyeti। mahārṇava-kathanenāśritasya sarve'pi doṣāstatra nimagrā  
bhavantīti dhvanyate। vastuto'anvarthatac vātsalyaṃ bhagavatyeva ghaṭate'nyatra tatprayogastu tad  
guṇaleśayogāndrākta eve ti padayapūrvārdhasya vivakṣitam। evambhūtavātsalyasya bhagavadekāśrayatāyā  
vaktari prāmāṇyamucyate - samucyata ityādayuttarārdhana। asvātantakairbhagavadadhīnatāpratyayavaddhiḥ  
sadācāraparāyaṇeḥ śāstavihita-karmatatparai varairśānato vayasā tapasā ca vṛddhatvaccharaṣainṛbhiriti।  
itthaṃ śāstaravaśyatvabuddhayā karmaniṣṭhā tatpravṛttiśca tayā ca varatvaṃutkṛṣṭatayā sarvamānyatvamiti mithī  
hetuparamparā, tena taduccyamānasyāvigītatvaṃ sūcyate। parāyaṇaśabdādeva nityāvalambatvapratītyā  
vibhīḥ sadā vātsalyamīṣṭamabhi-matamiyarthasiddhaye sadāśabdasya pūrvārdhi'nvaya ucitaḥ। yathā loke  
mātā vātsalyāt-putra-doṣānnavigaṇasya sahyati taccharīragatamalādikamapasāyayapikṣay  
vārliṣyati, tathaiva bhagavāñ śaraṇāgatajīvāṃstadradoṣopekṣayā tātrivārya vā svīkartuṃ vyagro  
"vatītyāśayaḥ। sarvadoṣa-nirākaraṇasya tad upekṣāyā vā sāmarthyasya bhagavaty eva satvādrātsalyaṃ  
tantarṣṭhameveti bhāvaḥ।  
dayā'nyaduḥkhasya nigadyate budheraprākṛtestairasahiṣṇutā smṛtā ।  
kṛpāmahāndheḥ samudārakīrtarviṣṇoracintyākhilavaibhavasya ve ।।108।।

Literal translation (with grammatical notes):

132 The shining one of the Sri Vaishnava doctrine,  
(the) heart-essence which surpasses the imperishable in one's own self; (hṛdayatvam yat oṣara-aukṣyātigaṃ nija)  
in the person, indeed, (there) would be (3rd person subjunctive) moreover (tattad dhi) affection (vātsalyaṃ) toward the devotee, of life and of Hari. (bhakte prāṇasya va hareḥ)  
The bond of possessive delusion (mamata-moha-sampakaḥ) is to be cherished among the children and others. (dradīyāṃ tanujādiṣu)  
And those who know possessive affection most excellent exalt deceit and mindlessness. (yap pi cchala-manaskatvaṃ vidur vātsalya-muttamāḥ)  
O dear one (vatsaḥ), that is full of affection, stable in the quality of fondness, is Hari — thus said. (srehaguṇa-sthamāstad-rattā vatsalo hariḥ' iti)  
With forbearance, the earth is equal (same) — thus (kṣamayā pṛthivī samaḥ' iti).  
Of the Blessed One's spoken forbearance, solely from supreme elder affection, (bhagavaty uktāyā kṣamāyāḥ parākāṣṭaiva vātsalyam ataḥ) by non-intervention, truly that is indeed called. (nāntarīyakatayā sā tu sutarameva ucyate)  
In place, (sthāne)  
he did (ca karah) together with earlier exposition-meaning (pūrvokti-arthana saha),  
dialogued (saṃvādītya) with established speech-being, therefore the Bhagavata likewise displays (vit vat tvādhagavato'pi vātsalyasyeti dyotayati) parental affection.  
It manifests the collection and arrangement of similar-category things. (tatsajātiya-vastu-gaṇa-samākalana-vyūyatāṃ ca dhvanayati).  
So also of affection is the eternal foundation said by the wise persons — that is called (vibhīḥ vātsalyamahārṇavasya iti padadrayena).  

Even there, first the dear one of the devotees established unceasingly (prathamaṃ priyo viputāṃ saṃsthāpya avyavadhānena),  
by the Blessed One also through qualification-gate (viśeṣaṇa-dvāreṇa) that principle is said: (tatvu-muccyate vibhī iti).  
The meaning is 'All-embodied-union' (sarva-mūrtha-saṃyogina ityarthaḥ),  
due to the separation of place, time, etc. (deśakālādya pariccheden), everywhere through lively excellence (sarvatra sphūrtim uttatvā),  
according to affection-related capability is spoken,  
for sure the patron from the same place is also not fruitful everywhere: (na hy akādeśī vatsalo'pi)  
in negation of other's defect at another place, in acceptance, or in preservation, such a one does not produce success everywhere. (deśāntarastha-janadoṣa-nirākaraṇe tad aṅgīkaraṇe tad rakṣaṇe vā prabhaved iti tādṛśasya)  
Forever with knowledge and pervasion is the Bhagavat fruit of that. (nitya jñānatvena sarvagatvena ca bhagavastat sapalaṃ bhavati).  

Not natural-born person, knowing the life’s birth, death, old age, and other afflictions, can remove them,  
nor ailments of husked rice age (tuṣataṇḍulavatte) are removable by small effort (svālpa-śrameṇa śakya pari hārā).  
Thus it is said: "He who protects the womb is protected." (sa rakṣitā rakṣati yo hi garbhaṃ) (Bhā.pu. 7.2.38).  
Of the venerables kind-hearted toward the lowly,  
they said, by purity of being and capacity with limitation, forever pure, with infinite capacity, even Bhagavat is infinite. (sattvaśuddheḥ sāmarthyasya ca parimitvena nityaśuddha - syānantasāmardhyasya bhagavataśca tad aparimeyam)  
Thus says the ocean of affection (vātsalyamahārṇavasya).  
By the narration of the great ocean, all faults indeed sink there (mahārṇava-kathanena āśritasya sarve'pi doṣāḥ tatra nimagrā bhavantīti dhvanyate).  
In reality also, by homogeneity, affection solely concentrates in Bhagavat, nowhere else; usage elsewhere is tainted with mixture of quality and passion, as explained before. (vastuto anvartha tac vātsalyaṃ bhagavatyeva ghaṭate anyatra tat prayogastu tad guṇa leśa yogān drākta eva iti padaya pūrvārdhasya vivakṣitam).  

Thus arisen affection’s sole support in Bhagavat’s speaking is declared authentic (evam bhūta vātsalyasya bhagavad eka-āśrayatāyā vaktari prāmāṇyam ucyate) —  
'exactly so' and similar is the latter half.  
By lack of independence, dependence on Bhagavat, faith bound,  
following good conduct, following prescribed scriptures and devoted to actions with select excellent qualities,  
by age and ascetic practice and unyielding vigor-like boldness —  
thus with scriptural constraint and intelligence, commitment to action and commencement and superior quality and universal acceptability, by this method of reasons, the above-mentioned ignorance is nullified (asvatantakaiḥ bhagavad adhīnatā pratyaya-vaddhiḥ sadācāra-parāyaṇeḥ śāstava-hita-karma tatpara ivaraiḥ śānato vayasā tapasā ca vṛddhatva ccharaṣainṛbhir iti).  

The word 'Parāyaṇa' itself, by eternal dependence, and the particle 'Vibhīḥ' always, fits well with the first half of the eternal word, for achieving the purpose of desired affection (parāyaṇa-śabdādeva nitya-āvalambatva-pratītyā vibhīḥ sadā vātsalyam īṣṭam abhi-matam ityartha-siddhaye sadāśabdasya pūrvārdhi anvaya ucitaḥ).  

As a mother in the world endures the son's defects due to affection and accepts the evil attached to the body such as impurity, decay, etc.,  
just so Bhagavan with refuge seekers among lives, by overlooking their defects, tolerates their refusal or acceptance with eagerness of intention (yathā loke mātā vātsalyāt putra-doṣān navigaṇasya sahyati tat śarīragata malādikam apasāyaya pīkṣaya vārl iṣyati tathāiva bhagavāñ śaraṇāgata jīvāṃs tadradoṣopekṣayā tātrivārya vā svīkartuṃ vyagro vatītyāśayaḥ).  

By neglect of all defect-removal or by capacity, affection in Bhagavat alone steadfast remains (sarva-doṣa nirākaraṇasya tad upekṣāyā vā sāmarthyasya bhagavaty eva sattvā drātsalyaṃ tantar iṣṭhameveti bhāvaḥ).  

Compassion and other sufferings are mentioned by the wise,  
by the natural and intolerant are remembered;  
of the great ocean (of) compassion of Vishnu, the inconceivable source of all opulence (kṛpā mahāndheḥ samudārakīrtar viṣṇor acintyākhila vaibhavasya ve).

Smooth English translation:

132 The radiant sun of the Sri Vaishnava doctrine —  
that pure heart-essence which surpasses all perishability in oneself;  
in a person, there surely arises affection toward the devotee, the life force, and Hari.  
The bond of possessive delusion is to be nurtured among children and others.  
Those who know the highest affection magnify deceit and lack of mind.  
O dear one, that fond and affectionate Hari, steady in loving qualities, is so described.  
With forbearance, even the earth is equal — so it is said.  
From the supreme affection inherent in the Blessed One’s forbearance, by non-intervention, it is truly called that affection unbroken.  
In this way, together with earlier teachings and dialogue, the Bhagavata manifests parental affection by arranged collection of like things.  
Thus, affection is declared by learned ones as the eternal support, called the great ocean of affection.  

Therein, the beloved devotee steadily established, the Blessed One also states through special qualifications this essential truth:  
The meaning is ‘union with all forms.’  
Due to separation by place and time, vivacity arises everywhere;  
affection suitably aligned with that capability is declared.  
Indeed, even the affectionate one native to a place is not everywhere effective;  
such one does not succeed in negating, accepting, or preserving defects of others elsewhere.  
The Bhagavat attains full fruit by eternal knowledge and omnipresence.  

No ordinary person can remove the pains of birth, death, and old age, nor do faults of ripe grain vanish with little effort.  
Therefore it is said, ‘He who protects the womb is protected.’  
To the venerables, kind toward the lowly, it is proclaimed:  
By purity and limited capacity, ever pure with infinite power, the Bhagavat is unlimited.  
So speaks the ocean of affection.  
All faults truly sink there by reference to the ocean’s narration.  
Indeed, affection belongs solely to the Bhagavat; elsewhere usage is tainted by combination of qualities and passion, as explained.  

Thus, the sole support of affection’s manifestation in the Bhagavat’s words is declared authentic and fitting by the commentator.  
Because of reliance on Bhagavat, bound faith, dedication to good conduct, and adherence to scriptural action  
with vigor, age, and asceticism, enhanced by courage and steadiness:  
Through reasoned method, ignorance about this is dispelled.  

The term ‘Parāyaṇa’ itself, indicating constant dependence, and ‘Vibhīḥ’ always, are appropriate for the first half of that eternal word, confirming the understood meaning of affection desired.  

As a mother tolerates her son’s defects for the sake of affection, accepting the impurities and illnesses of the body,  
so does Bhagavan overlook faults of refuge-seekers, willingly permitting even their refusal or acceptance.  

By capacity to overlook all fault-removal or by sheer ability, affection steadfastly abides with Bhagavat alone.  

Compassion and suffering are described by wise ones,  
recognized by those natural and uncompromising,  
as the great compassionate ocean of Vishnu, the inconceivable source of all opulence.
